Taxi vs Train from Tonbridge to Heathrow: An Honest Comparison

By the Southborough Airport Taxis team · Published 2 April 2025 · Updated 15 November 2026

Short answer

From Tonbridge, the train to Heathrow involves travelling into London and changing at least once, typically taking two hours or more door to door. A fixed-price taxi takes around 60–85 minutes via the A21 and M25 and costs from £107 for up to four passengers, so for two or more travellers, early flights or heavy luggage the taxi is usually the better option.

Journey time

By road, Tonbridge to Heathrow is around 55 miles, mostly on the A21 and M25. By rail you travel into London first and then out again to Heathrow, usually with one or two changes. Once you add the walk or drive to Tonbridge station and waiting time between trains, the rail journey is typically longer than the taxi.

Cost for one, two and four travellers

Rail fares are per person and vary with time of day and how far ahead you book. A taxi fare is per vehicle, so the cost per person falls as more people share. For a solo traveller with a backpack, an off-peak train is often cheapest; for a couple or a family, a shared fixed-price taxi is frequently similar or cheaper.

Luggage and early starts

Carrying large cases through London stations at rush hour is hard work, and the earliest trains may not get you to Heathrow in time for a 7am departure. A taxi collects you from your door at whatever time you need and drops you at your terminal.

Our verdict

Take the train if you are travelling alone, light and off-peak. Book a taxi if you are travelling as a group, have heavy luggage, need an early or late connection, or simply want a door-to-door journey with no changes.
